Paste a Nigerian phone number in any format. Get back its normalised local and international form, and the network its prefix was originally allocated to by the NCC.

What this checks, and what it can't. Format validity and original NCC prefix allocation only. Not location, call history, or live status — that data sits with the carriers under lawful-intercept rules, and no public tool can reach it. Numbers you type never leave this page.

How the check works

  1. 1
    Paste. Any spacing, dashes, brackets or a leading +234 all parse the same way.
  2. 2
    Normalise. Reduced to an 11-digit local MSISDN and its +234 E.164 equivalent.
  3. 3
    Match. The 4-digit prefix is looked up against the NCC's original block allocation.

Operator prefixes reflect the NCC's original block allocations to MTN, Globacom, Airtel and 9mobile — public reference data, not a live carrier lookup. Numbers are portable between networks, so the operator shown is a number's origin, not necessarily where it lives today.
